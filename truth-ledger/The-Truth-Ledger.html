49 days to become a man whose word holds.

Read this first. This guide is built on the methods counselors use for compulsive lying: tracking the behavior (CBT), finding the fear that drives it, building a pause, practicing small truths (exposure), repair, and relapse planning. It is a daily structure, not therapy. Do it alongside a licensed counselor; booking one is on Day 3. If you ever feel hopeless or think about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US).

Tonight: Day 0

Don't wait for Monday. Do these five things before you sleep.

  1. Write one sentence: "I am a man whose word holds, even when the truth costs me."
  2. Count honestly: roughly how many lies did you tell this week? Write the number.
  3. Find 2 or 3 licensed counselors and write their names. You'll book one by Day 3.
  4. Choose your accountability brother and text him: "I'm working on something. Can I send you my numbers once a week?"
  5. If she's open to it, tell your wife: "I've lied to you, and it comes from fear. I'm not asking you to trust me. I've started daily work and I'm booking a counselor this week. You'll see it, not just hear it."

Then do not tell another lie tonight.

The three lines

  1. No new lies. When the alarm goes off in your body, pause. Three seconds and one breath, then a true sentence.
  2. Correct it fast. If one gets out, fix it within 60 seconds, or the same day at the latest: “Actually, that's not true.”
  3. Log every one, every night. Being honest with the ledger is the first honesty. A ledger with a lie in it is worthless.

The pause: true sentences to use

A lie fills a silence. Have true sentences ready so you have something else to say.

When the urge hits“Give me a second. I want to answer that honestly.”
When you don't know“I don't know.” / “I'm not sure. Let me check and tell you.”
When you didn't do it“I didn't do it yet. I'll have it done by ___.”
When you failed“I messed that up. Here's what I'm doing about it.”
When it already slipped out“Actually, that's not true. The truth is ___.”
When you're ashamed“I'm embarrassed to tell you this, but…”
When she asks and you need time“I'll answer that fully. Give me until tonight.” Then answer it tonight.

The evening ledger

Use the Notes app or a paper notebook, one page per night. Numbers matter. Over 49 days you should see lies told go down while lies caught and corrections go up.

THE LEDGER (10 min)
1. Lies told today (#): what, to whom
2. Lies caught before I spoke (#)
3. Corrections made (#)
4. The trigger: who, where, and the fear (failure / rejection / conflict / looking weak)
5. The story in my head: "If I tell the truth, ___ will happen."
6. What actually happened when I told the truth
7. Tomorrow's risk moment and the true sentence I'll say
8. One line of prayer or thanks

With your wife

  • Don't dump everything in a panic tonight. A rushed, emotional confession usually comes out in pieces and causes more damage. Plan the full truth with your counselor (Week 5).
  • Don't add any new lies. If she asks you something directly, don't lie. If you need time: “I'll answer that fully. I want to do it right, with the counselor.” Then follow through.
  • No trickle truth. Telling the truth a little at a time, each time you're caught, breaks trust again every time. One full disclosure is far better.
  • Don't ask for trust. Earn it. Never ask “When will you trust me?” Give her things she can check: kept promises, open access, your weekly numbers if she wants them.
  • Her pain isn't an attack. Stay in the room, listen, and don't argue about the details.
  • Consider couples counseling once your own work has started. Your counselor can tell you when.

Week 1

See It

You can't fix what you won't count.

Compulsive lying runs on autopilot. This week you don't fix anything; you turn the lights on. Counting is the first honest thing you do.

“Whoever conceals their sins does not prosper, but the one who confesses and renounces them finds mercy.” Proverbs 28:13

Day 1

The count begins

Morning. Today you only count. Every lie, every exaggeration, every "yeah, I did" that wasn't true. No fixing, no beating yourself up. Keep a tally in your phone notes as it happens.

Tonight. How many did you log? Which one surprised you most, and why?

Day 2

Name the kind

Morning. Tag each lie today by type. COVER: hiding a mistake. INFLATE: looking better than you are. SMOOTH: avoiding conflict. AUTO: no reason at all, it just came out.

Tonight. Which type showed up most? What does that tell you that you're protecting?

Day 3

Book the counselor

Morning. Action before noon: book a licensed counselor (LPC, LMFT or psychologist) who works with shame, anxiety or couples. Ask your pastor for a referral or search a directory. Put the first appointment on your calendar.

Tonight. Is it booked? If not, what did you tell yourself about why not, and was that true?

Day 4

Who gets the lies

Morning. Notice who you lie to most. Write their names as it happens: wife, boss, kids, friends, parents, yourself.

Tonight. Who is it hardest to tell the truth to? What do you fear they'll think of you?

Day 5

The alarm in your body

Morning. Notice what your body does right before a lie: tight chest, hot face, dry throat, talking faster. That feeling is your alarm. Learn it today.

Tonight. Describe your alarm. How many seconds were there between the alarm and the lie?

Day 6

The cost

Morning. Write a specific list of what lying has cost you: trust, peace, money, time, intimacy, sleep, respect for yourself. Names and moments, not general ideas.

Tonight. Which cost are you no longer willing to pay?

Day 7 · Review

Week 1 review

Morning. Tonight is your first weekly review. Today, keep counting. Also pick your accountability brother: one man you will text your numbers to every week.

Tonight. Total the week: lies told, caught, corrected. Text your brother the three numbers with no excuses attached. What pattern do you see?

Week 2

The Fear Underneath

Every lie is protecting something.

You told us why you lie: fear of failing and fear of not being accepted. This week you find exactly where those fears live and what they've been buying you.

“There is no fear in love. But perfect love drives out fear.” 1 John 4:18

Day 8

The fear of failing

Morning. Each time you feel the urge to lie today, ask yourself: "Am I hiding that I failed?"

Tonight. What failure are you most ashamed for people to see? Where did you learn that failing makes you less of a man?

Day 9

The fear of not being accepted

Morning. Each time the urge hits, ask yourself: "Am I trying to be liked right now?"

Tonight. Finish this sentence ten times without stopping: "If they knew the real me, they would..."

Day 10

Where it started

Morning. At some point today, think back to the first time you remember lying to stay safe. Who were you protecting yourself from?

Tonight. Write that boy a short letter. What did he need to hear that he never heard? Bring it to your counselor.

Day 11

Shame vs. guilt

Morning. Guilt says "I did something wrong." Shame says "I am wrong." Shame drives lies. Catch shame talk in your head today and write it down.

Tonight. Rewrite one shame sentence as a guilt sentence: "I did ___, and I can make it right by ___."

Day 12

The story in your head

Morning. Before one hard truth today, write your prediction first: "If I say it, ___ will happen." Then say it.

Tonight. Did the prediction come true? Score it: worse, the same, or better than you predicted.

Day 13

What lying pays you

Morning. Be honest: lying pays. It buys relief, approval, time and escape. Each time today, name the payoff you were after.

Tonight. Which payoff will you have to grieve to give this up?

Day 14 · Review

Week 2 review: your Fear Map

Morning. Today, draw your Fear Map: your top three fears, the lie each one produces, and the truth each one avoids. Bring it to counseling.

Tonight. Total the week and text your brother. Compare it with Week 1. Which fear on the map is running your life the most?

Week 3

The Pause

Lies are fast. Truth needs three seconds.

The lie usually leaves your mouth before you've decided anything. This week you build the gap between the urge and the words, and you fill it with true sentences you've written ahead of time.

“Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry.” James 1:19

Day 15

The three-second pause

Morning. Practice the sequence: alarm, breath, "Give me a second." Use it at least once today, even in a conversation that's easy.

Tonight. How many times did you pause? What happened in those three seconds?

Day 16

"I don't know"

Morning. Say "I don't know" or "I'm not sure" at least once today instead of filling the silence with something you made up.

Tonight. How did it feel not to have an answer? Did anyone actually think less of you?

Day 17

The 60-second correction

Morning. If a lie slips out, correct it inside a minute: "Actually, that's not true. The truth is ___." Fast correction beats a perfect record.

Tonight. Did you correct one? If not, which lie from today can you still correct tomorrow? Put it on tomorrow's list.

Day 18

Write your scripts

Morning. Write five true sentences for your five most common lie situations ("Did you finish it?" "Where were you?" "How much did it cost?"). Save them in your phone.

Tonight. Which script is hardest to say out loud? Say it out loud right now, three times.

Day 19

Slow down

Morning. In every hard conversation today, speak about 20% slower. Rushing feeds lies.

Tonight. Where did slowing down change what came out of your mouth?

Day 20

No rounding up

Morning. Today, nothing gets inflated: not numbers, times, effort or stories. Exact is the goal.

Tonight. Where did you want to round up? Who were you trying to be in that moment?

Day 21 · Review

Week 3 review

Morning. Read your scripts once this morning. Ask your counselor whether it's time to let your wife see your weekly numbers.

Tonight. Total the week and text your brother. Is your pause count going up while your lie count goes down?

Week 4

Small Truths, Out Loud

Courage is a muscle. Train it light first.

Counselors call this exposure: you practice the thing you fear in small, planned steps until your body learns that the truth doesn't kill you.

“Therefore each of you must put off falsehood and speak truthfully to your neighbor.” Ephesians 4:25

Day 22

Build your truth ladder

Morning. List ten truths you've been avoiding, from easiest ("I'm running late") to hardest. Today, tell the easiest one.

Tonight. Rate your fear from 0 to 10 before you said it and after. What changed?

Day 23

Disappoint someone on purpose

Morning. Say one honest "no" or "I can't" today with no made-up excuse attached.

Tonight. What happened? Did the relationship survive the truth?

Day 24

Admit it first

Morning. Before anyone finds out, admit one small mistake today: "That was me. I'll fix it."

Tonight. How did it land? Compare what it cost with what you feared it would cost.

Day 25

Money and work

Morning. Money and work attract lies. Tell one true thing today about money or work that you've been fudging.

Tonight. What's still hidden in money or work that needs to come out? Write it down for your counselor.

Day 26

Climb a rung

Morning. Tell rung three or four of your ladder today.

Tonight. Rate your fear before and after. What's getting easier?

Day 27

Take the reaction

Morning. When a truth gets a reaction today (frustration, hurt, silence), don't defend, explain or lie again. Say: "You're right to feel that."

Tonight. Where did you want to defend yourself? What did you do instead?

Day 28 · Review

Week 4 review

Morning. Look at your ladder. Mark every rung you've climbed.

Tonight. Total the week and text your brother. Which truth did you think would wreck you but didn't?

Week 5

Repair

Telling the truth is the start. Repair is the work.

This is the week the work turns toward your wife. It is done carefully, with your counselor, and never in pieces.

“Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed.” James 5:16

Day 29

The inventory

Morning. Start a private, written inventory of the lies that have affected your wife. Facts only, no excuses. It's for your counselor first. This is not a midnight confession.

Tonight. What's the hardest item on the list? What do you fear will happen if she knows it?

Day 30

No trickle truth

Morning. Know this: telling the truth in pieces, a little more each time you're caught, does more damage than the original lie. Ask your counselor to help you plan one full, honest disclosure.

Tonight. Have you asked your counselor about disclosure? If not, when exactly will you?

Day 31

Her side

Morning. Today, try to see it from her side. What has it been like to live with a man whose word she couldn't count on?

Tonight. Write one page in her voice about what she has felt. Don't send it. Read it twice.

Day 32

A real apology

Morning. A real apology has five parts: what I did, how it hurt you, no excuses, what I'm doing about it, and no demand to be forgiven. Draft one.

Tonight. Read your draft and delete every "but," "if" and "I was just." What's left?

Day 33

Answer the whole question

Morning. When she asks you something, answer it fully the first time. If you need time: "I'll answer that fully. Give me until tonight." Then do it.

Tonight. Did you answer anything only partway today? Finish it.

Day 34

Repair with action

Morning. Pick one lie you can repair with action: return the money, keep the promise, finish the task. Do it today.

Tonight. What did the repair cost you? Was it worth it?

Day 35 · Review

Week 5 review

Morning. Check with your counselor: are you ready for disclosure, and what's the plan?

Tonight. Total the week and text your brother. What has repair taught you that confession alone couldn't?

Week 6

Transparency

Trust is rebuilt on boring days.

She can't trust your words yet, so give her things she can check. Trust comes back through small, verifiable, repeated evidence.

“Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much.” Luke 16:10

Day 36

Get ahead of it

Morning. Tell her something before she asks: your schedule, a purchase, a mistake.

Tonight. What did you volunteer? How did it feel to be ahead of the truth instead of behind it?

Day 37

Open book

Morning. Offer access without being asked: phone, accounts, calendar, whatever was hidden before. Offer it as evidence, not as punishment.

Tonight. What are you still keeping in a back room?

Day 38

Her anger isn't the enemy

Morning. If she's hurt or angry today, it's the cost of what happened, not an attack on you. Stay in the room, listen, and don't argue about the facts.

Tonight. Did you stay? At what moment did you want to leave?

Day 39

Don't demand trust

Morning. Don't ask "When are you going to trust me?" Whether she trusts you is her decision. Being consistent is yours.

Tonight. What did you do today that she could check for herself?

Day 40

Keep one small promise

Morning. Make one small, specific promise with a time on it, and keep it exactly.

Tonight. Did you keep it, on time? Small promises kept are how your words become believable again.

Day 41

Say the feeling

Morning. Many lies stand in for feelings. Today, say one feeling out loud: "I'm afraid." "I'm embarrassed." "I'm stressed about money."

Tonight. What feeling did you finally say? What happened next?

Day 42 · Review

Week 6 review

Morning. Ask your wife one question today, and only if she's open to it: "What's one thing that would help you feel safer with me this week?" Just listen.

Tonight. Total the week and text your brother. What did she tell you, and what will you do about it?

Week 7

A Man Whose Word Holds

Not a streak. An identity.

You finish by deciding who you are, planning for the day you slip, and choosing what keeps running after Day 49.

“All you need to say is simply 'Yes' or 'No.'” Matthew 5:37

Day 43

Your identity sentence

Morning. Rewrite your vision sentence: "I am a man who tells the truth when it costs me." Read it three times this morning.

Tonight. Where did you live that sentence today?

Day 44

Your warning signs

Morning. Write your relapse warning signs: tired, stressed, money trouble, isolated, skipping the ledger, keeping "small" secrets.

Tonight. Which warning sign is showing right now?

Day 45

Your relapse plan

Morning. Write it and sign it: If I lie, I will 1) correct it the same day, 2) log it, 3) tell my brother within 24 hours, 4) bring it to counseling.

Tonight. Who has a copy? Send it to your brother tonight.

Day 46

Teach it

Morning. How you handle your own mistakes teaches your kids how to handle theirs. Admit a mistake in front of them today, in a way that fits their age.

Tonight. What do you want your children to learn about truth from watching you?

Day 47

What the truth gave you

Morning. List five things that seven weeks of truth have given you.

Tonight. Thank your wife for one specific thing she has done during this. Thank God for one too.

Day 48

Your ongoing system

Morning. Decide what keeps running after Day 49. Keep: the nightly ledger, the Sunday review, counseling until your counselor says otherwise, and a weekly text to your brother. Optional: the midday check.

Tonight. Set those reminders for the next 90 days before you sleep.

Day 49 · Review

Day 49

Morning. Read your Day 1 numbers this morning.

Tonight. Write a letter to the man who started on Day 1. Then tell your wife one thing you've learned about yourself.

If you lie (and on some day you will)

A slip isn't a relapse. Hiding the slip is the relapse.

  1. Correct it the same day.
  2. Log it honestly tonight.
  3. Tell your brother within 24 hours.
  4. Bring it to counseling.
  5. Start the next day where you are. Don't go back to Day 1.

Set up the reminders

  1. Download install-truth-ledger.command to your Mac's Downloads folder.
  2. Open Terminal and run: bash ~/Downloads/install-truth-ledger.command (add today at the end to make today Day 1).
  3. Allow Terminal to control Reminders when your Mac asks.
  4. Check that the list syncs to your iPhone: Settings → your name → iCloud → Reminders is on.

No Mac nearby? Open Truth-Ledger.ics on your iPhone to add the same schedule to Calendar, with alerts.
