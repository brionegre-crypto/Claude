How it works

Four steps. Each one ends in something you can check.

Scripture sets the standard. The structure handles the follow-through. It's built around how men actually change: from still wondering, to ready to plan, to trying and slipping, to keeping it.

1

Step 1 · Free · The Calling

Write who God has called you to be.

List your roles: son of God, husband or future husband, father or future father, son, worker, brother. For each one ask, "What does God expect of me here?" Use Scripture, not feelings. Then write one sentence: "I am a man who ___, so that ___."

You leave with: one identity sentence.

"Write the vision; make it plain." Habakkuk 2:2

2

Step 2 · The Commitments

Turn it into three things you can check.

For each role, write what that sentence requires this week. Then cut the list to three, no more. Each one must be a yes or no with a number. Decide what counts as a miss before the week starts.

You leave with: no more than three written commitments across faith, home, health and work.

"Let what you say be simply Yes or No." Matthew 5:37

3

Step 3 · The Floor

Give each one a time, a place and a bad-day version.

Name what will break each commitment: time slipping, evening fatigue, the phone, shame. Then lock it to a day, a time, a place and a trigger: "Thursday, 7:40 PM, phone in the kitchen drawer."

Finally, write the two-minute version you will still do on your worst day, so one bad Wednesday bends the plan instead of ending it.

You leave with: a trigger and a floor for every commitment.

"The righteous falls seven times and rises again." Proverbs 24:16

4

Step 4 · The Ledger

Score your week with other men.

Every week you mark each commitment done or missed, report the number to the men in your group, take grace without shame, and reset for Monday. Four out of seven is four out of seven.

You leave with: a weekly number, for example 13 of 15 kept.

"One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much." Luke 16:10

"Iron sharpens iron." Proverbs 27:17
