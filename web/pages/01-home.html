God at the center

Willpower isn't the plan.

Good intentions don't change a week, and for a brain that loses time and drops the thread they change it even less. A plan you can see does.

A goal

I'll be more present.

You meant it. Nothing that week changed.

A system

Thursday, 7:40 PM, phone in the drawer.

When, where and on what trigger, written down so it can be checked.

Wrote down when and where91%
Didn't38%

In one study, 91% of people who wrote down when and where they would act followed through, against 38% who didn't. Across 94 studies, the pattern held. (That research is about written plans in general, not about ADHD or autism in particular.)

Your brain doesn't have to remember it. The page does, the calendar does, a brother does. And the center isn't you: the man in Step 1 is the man God is making, and the standard is scripture, not mood.

Built around how your brain works.

Put it where you can see it

One written sentence. One page you can look at. “Write the vision; make it plain on tablets, so he may run who reads it.” Habakkuk 2:2

Give it a time and a place

“This week” is not a time. “Thursday, 7:40 PM” is. Time is easier to feel when it has an address.

Make a bad-day version

The smallest version that still counts, so a rough week bends the plan instead of ending it.
