## How to read this

The evidence below is for the **methods** each product uses: writing a plan with a time, tracking progress on paper, praying for your spouse, and so on. It is **not** evidence that these specific products work. No one has run a study on them, and there are no customer results yet.

So the site copy always says **"built on"** or **"designed around"** a method, and never "proven" or "guaranteed." There are two reasons:

1. **It's true.** The brand's Evidence Law says "numbers as numbers, not stories." Claiming a study about a method proves your product would break that law.
2. **It's required.** The FTC requires advertising claims to be backed by evidence for the thing being sold. A claim like "proven to save marriages" needs a study of *your* product.

**How each source is labeled:**
- **Experiment:** people were randomly assigned to groups, so the study can show cause.
- **Meta-analysis:** a study that combines many studies. This is the strongest kind.
- **Observational:** the study found a link, not cause and effect. The copy has to say "linked to," never "causes."

## Product by product: what the evidence shows and the copy to use

Each entry has the result the product aims for, the evidence behind its method, and the exact copy for the site.

### Tier 0: Step 1 worksheet and the framework

- **Method:** implementation intentions. You write down *when, where and what* you'll do, not just the goal.
- **Evidence:**
  - Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) combined **94 tests** and found a **medium-to-large effect** (d = 0.65) on reaching goals. *(Meta-analysis.)*
  - In one of those experiments, **91%** of people who wrote down the day, time and place they'd exercise did it that week, against **35–38%** of people who didn't plan (Milne, Orbell & Sheeran, 2002). *(Experiment.)*
- **Site copy:** "A goal says what. A plan says when. In one study, 91% of people who wrote down the day, time and place followed through, against 38% who didn't. Step 1 is where you write yours."

> **Applied 2026-10-03 (saved in Canva, live on next Publish):** it says *"Across 94 studies, people who wrote down when and where they would act were roughly three times more likely to follow through."* The 94-study review didn't find "three times"; it found a medium-to-large effect. The roughly-three-times number comes from the single 2002 exercise study (91% vs 35–38%). Replace it with: *"Across 94 studies, writing down when and where you'll act made people measurably more likely to follow through. In one, 91% of planners did it, against 38% of non-planners."*

### Weekly Scorecard ($7)

- **Method:** tracking progress on paper and showing it to someone.
- **Evidence:** Harkin et al. (2016) looked at **138 experiments with 19,951 people**. Tracking your progress made people more likely to reach their goals (d = 0.40). The effect was **larger when the progress was written down and when it was reported to someone else**. *(Meta-analysis, Psychological Bulletin.)*
- **Site copy:** "Across 138 experiments with almost 20,000 people, those who tracked their progress reached more of their goals. The effect was strongest when the record was on paper and shared with someone. The Scorecard does both: one page, built to show to one person."

### Forty Cards for Men ($9 proposed)

- **Method:** one specific action a day, tied to a set time. That's how habits form.
- **Evidence:**
  - Lally et al. (2010) found it took a **median of 66 days** (range 18 to 254) for a daily behavior to become automatic. *(Observational, 96 adults.)*
  - Implementation-intentions research is above.
- **Site copy:** "Habits take about two months of daily repetition to become automatic, and sometimes longer. Forty cards, one action each, gets you more than halfway."

### Christian Couples Conversation Cards ($9)

- **Method:** structured questions that get gradually more personal.
- **Evidence:**
  - Aron et al. (1997): pairs who answered questions that slowly became more personal felt significantly closer than pairs who made small talk. *(Experiment. Participants were strangers, not married couples.)*
  - Gottman's newlywed research: couples still married six years later responded to each other's bids for attention **86%** of the time; couples who divorced responded **33%** of the time. *(Observational. Reported by the Gottman Institute.)*
- **Site copy:** "Questions that get a little deeper each time build closeness; that's been shown in the lab. And the couples who last keep turning toward each other: 86% of the time in one long-term study, against 33% for couples who split. Twenty minutes, phones away."

### The Husband's Devotional ($9) and The Prayer Strategy for Men ($9)

- **Method:** praying specifically for your spouse, every day.
- **Evidence:** Fincham, Lambert & Beach (2010, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*), three studies:
  - People **randomly assigned to pray for their partner daily for four weeks** were less unfaithful during that time than people assigned to think positive thoughts about their partner.
  - Outside observers rated the people who prayed as **more committed**.
  - *(Experiment. Participants were college students in romantic relationships, not married couples.)*
- **Site copy:** "In a randomized study, people who prayed for their partner every day for four weeks were less unfaithful during that time than people who thought positive thoughts about their partner. Observers rated them as more committed. Prayer did more than positive thinking. This tool gives you the names, the requests and the dates."

### Godly Husband Field Guide ($19) and Christian Marriage Workbook for Men ($19)

- **Method:** structured relationship-skills training, plus planned weekly time as a couple.
- **Evidence:**
  - Hawkins et al. (2008): across **117 studies**, marriage education programs improved communication skills (d ≈ 0.43–0.45) and relationship quality (d ≈ 0.30–0.36). Longer programs did better than short ones. *(Meta-analysis.)*
  - Wilcox & Dew (2012): couples who spent time together **at least once a week were about 3.5 times more likely** to report a very happy marriage. *(Observational, about 1,600 couples. Couples who are already happy may simply spend more time together.)*
- **Site copy:** "Marriage education works: across 117 studies, structured programs measurably improved couples' communication and relationship quality, and longer programs did better than one-off advice. This workbook runs eight sessions. Weekly time as a couple is linked to being about 3.5 times as likely to say the marriage is very happy."

### Father's Field Guide ($19) and Father and Son Conversation Cards ($9 proposed)

- **Method:** direct, regular engagement with your child, not just being in the house.
- **Evidence:** Sarkadi et al. (2008) reviewed **16 long-term studies covering over 22,000 children**. Fathers who were actively engaged were linked to fewer behavior problems in boys, fewer psychological problems in young women, and better cognitive development. *(Systematic review of observational studies.)*
- **Site copy:** "Sixteen long-term studies of more than 22,000 children found that what matters is a father's direct engagement, not just being in the house. It's linked to fewer behavior problems and stronger development. This guide turns engagement into decisions with a time attached."

### Men's Bible Study ($19) and Men's Discipleship Workbook ($19)

- **Method:** faith practiced together with others, regularly.
- **Evidence:** Li, Kubzansky & VanderWeele (2018) followed **66,444 married nurses for 14 years**. Those who attended religious services more than once a week were about **50% less likely to divorce**. *(Observational, and all participants were women. It shows a link, not cause.)*
- **Site copy:** "In a 14-year study of more than 66,000 married adults, those who regularly practiced their faith with others were about half as likely to divorce. This study is built for eight men to work through together."
- **Note:** this is the weakest link between a study and a product in the list, because the study is about church attendance, not Bible study. Use it softly or leave it out.

### Tier 3: The Club

- **Method:** support that continues past the first few weeks, with progress reported to other men.
- **Evidence:** habits take a median of **66 days**, and up to 254 (Lally 2010). Tracking works best when it's **reported to others** (Harkin 2016).
- **Site copy:** "A new habit takes about two months to stick, and for some men much longer. A one-time download ends before that. The Club is built for month three: every tool, a new one each month, and men who see your Scorecard."

### Tier 4: 6-week live group

- **Method:** a structured program of moderate length, with weekly reporting to a small group.
- **Evidence:** Hawkins (2008) found longer, structured programs did better than short ones. Harkin (2016) found reporting progress to others strengthens the effect of tracking. Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) support written when-and-where plans.
- **Site copy:** "Three things the research keeps finding: written plans with a time attached, progress you track on paper, and progress reported to other people. Six Sundays, four men, all three."

### Tier 5: Couples (certified marriage counseling)

> **Rule:** the certifying organization and its assessment are never named on the site or in ads. The copy says "a validated relationship assessment" and uses only the results.

- **Method:** a validated couples assessment, followed by feedback sessions.
- **Evidence:**
  - Fowers & Olson (1986) and Larsen & Olson (1989): Scores on the assessment Brian is certified to use, taken before the wedding, identified which couples later divorced and which were happily married with **about 80–85% accuracy**. *(Studies published by the assessment's own developers.)*
  - Stanley, Amato, Johnson & Markman (2006), *Journal of Family Psychology*: premarital education was linked to **about 30% lower odds of divorce**, plus higher satisfaction and less conflict. *(Observational, large random household survey.)*
- **Site copy:** "Certified marriage counseling, built on a validated assessment. Before the wedding, the validated assessment we use has picked out which couples later thrived and which divorced with about 80–85% accuracy. Couples who did premarital education were about 30% less likely to divorce. Three sessions, built on your own results."

**Two things to settle before the page goes live:**
1. **Don't name the certifying organization.** Brian's rule: the copy says "certified" and gives the results, but never the organization's or the assessment's name.
2. **Confirm the Alabama rules once.** Alabama's licensing law (Ala. Code §34-17A) restricts titles like "marriage counselor" to licensed therapists, and clergy have an exemption for counseling done as part of ministry. The wording above describes the *service* rather than giving you a licensed *title*. Since the couples service runs separately from the church, a quick check with the Alabama Board of Examiners in Marriage and Family Therapy before launch is worth the five minutes.

## Building your own evidence (starts with the 6-week group)

By December 20 you can have results that come from your own men, not borrowed studies:

- **Week 1 and week 6:** each man scores his Scorecard and answers one question: "Could someone in your house tell something changed?" (1 to 10).
- **Week 6:** ask for written permission to quote him, using first name and last initial only.
- **Report numbers as numbers:** for example, "4 of 4 men kept the Scorecard for six weeks" or "average house-test score rose from 4 to 7." With four men, say "in our first group," not "proven."

After two or three groups, the site can say **"Results from our groups"** next to the research.
