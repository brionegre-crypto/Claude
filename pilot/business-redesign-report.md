## 2. The specific problem you solve

**The problem:** A man who loves God, means what he says, and has ADHD keeps failing to turn good intentions into follow-through. He starts strong and stalls by Wednesday. He drops the plan the day one thing breaks. Then shame sets in. He has been told the answer is prayer, discipline and willpower. None of those is a system built for his brain.

**The claim you can make honestly:** it is a plan problem, not a faith problem. Give him a written plan, a time and a place, a bad-day version, and men who check in.

**What you do not say:** that you treat, cure, manage or diagnose ADHD. You are faith-based coaching and education, not medical treatment.

## 4. The system: four steps

Why four and not five or seven: ADHD brains lose track of long lists. Four is memorable and each step has one deliverable. The bad-day Floor is your differentiator, so it gets its own step. The old "Friction Audit" folds into Step 3.

| Step | Name | What he does | Deliverable | Scripture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Calling | Writes who God has called him to be in each role | One identity sentence | Habakkuk 2:2 |
| 2 | The Commitments | Turns that sentence into no more than three weekly commitments across faith, home, health, work | Three written commitments | Matthew 5:37 |
| 3 | The Floor | Names what will break each commitment, gives it a day, time, place and trigger, and writes a 2-minute bad-day version | A trigger and a floor for each commitment | Proverbs 24:16 |
| 4 | The Ledger | Scores the week, reports it to his brothers, takes grace without shame, resets for Monday | A weekly scorecard (for example, 13 of 15 kept) | Luke 16:10 and Proverbs 27:17 |

**Four Pillars (Faith, Home, Health, Work)** and **Six Laws** are the operating rules taught inside steps 2 to 4. They are not separate steps.

### How a man builds his own identity (Step 1, 20 minutes)

1. List his roles: son of God, husband or future husband, father or future father, son, worker or builder, brother in his community.
2. For each role, answer: "What does God expect of me here?" Use Scripture, not feelings.
3. Answer: "What does a faithful day in this role look like on a Tuesday?"
4. Write one sentence: "I am a man who ___, so that ___."
5. Read it aloud. Keep it on paper where he sees it.

### How he turns that into goals (Step 2, about 30 minutes)

1. For each role, write one thing that sentence requires this week.
2. Cut the list to three. If it has more than three, it will collapse.
3. Each commitment must be checkable: a yes or no, with a number.
4. Write what counts as a miss before the week starts.

### Teaching ours versus helping them build their own

Do both. You supply a fixed frame (the four steps, the Scorecard, the Floor template, the live review). He supplies the contents (his sentence, his goals, his triggers, his floor). That is more credible than "do it my way" and more effective than "make your own system" with no structure.

### Where Prochaska and DiClemente fit

Use the Stages of Change (precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance) as **internal design logic**, not as a public claim:

| Step | Stage it supports |
|---|---|
| 1 The Calling | Contemplation into preparation |
| 2 The Commitments | Preparation |
| 3 The Floor | Action and relapse prevention |
| 4 The Ledger | Maintenance |

How you use it:
- Ask each new man where he is (still wondering, ready to plan, already trying and slipping) and tailor the first call.
- Teach that relapse is expected and the plan has a floor.
- Public wording: "Built around how men actually change." Never say "proven by Stages of Change." Evidence that matching programs to stage improves outcomes is mixed, and it is not research on Be The Man itself.
- Book to read: *Changing for Good* by Prochaska, Norcross and DiClemente.

## 7. How to win with zero exposure

Zero audience means zero reach. You compensate with conversations and a clear promise.

1. **20 conversations in 14 days (customer discovery).** Talk to men who fit. Use *The Mom Test*: ask about their past ("Tell me about the last time a plan fell apart"), not about your idea. Goal: language to use, and your first buyers.
2. **Warm first.** Your phone contacts, your church-adjacent relationships (without naming any church), former counseling referrals only if permitted, family, men's groups you already belong to. Ask: "Who do you know who keeps starting strong and stalling?"
3. **Be present where the men already are.** ADHD adult Facebook groups, Christian men's groups, fraternity and barbershop circles, gyms. Give help before you pitch. Read each group's rules.
4. **Offer the free live "Step 1 night".** Your existing Wednesday workshop can become this: free, 60 minutes, everyone finishes Step 1. It is the best conversion event you have.
5. **Pitch yourself as a guest** to the small Christian ADHD podcasts above and to men's podcasts. A 20-minute guest spot reaches someone else's audience.
6. **A founding offer with a reason to say yes now.** Ten seats, a guarantee (your 30-day refund), and a permissioned testimonial in exchange for the discounted seats.

**Direct message script (adapt, do not spam)**
> Hey [name], I'm Brian. I run a small group where men write their plan, give every goal a time and place, and check in weekly. It's built for guys who keep starting strong and stalling. Not therapy, just structure and brothers. If that sounds like you or someone you know, I'll send you Step 1, free. Want it?

Rules: personal, short, one ask, no hype, never claim results you do not have. Send 10 a day, not 100.

**Paid ads.** Not yet. Meta restricts ads that assert or imply race, religion or a health condition. Write any ad as a situation ("Started the plan again and stalled again?") and recheck current policy.

## 9. 30-day "see my journey" YouTube pathway

Concept: **"30 days of keeping my word on paper."** You run the four steps yourself, on camera, and post your Scorecard honestly, misses included. Do not claim ADHD for yourself unless it is true. Say you are a pastor and counselor, not a clinician. If you have ADHD, tell that story in your words, it is the strongest asset this brand can have.

Format: 1 main video a week (8 to 12 minutes), 3 to 4 Shorts a week cut from it. Always end with one action: "Download Step 1. Link below."

| Week | Theme | Long video | Shorts ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Calling | "Pray harder is not a plan" (why willpower is the wrong tool) | "What Habakkuk 2:2 actually asks of you", "Write this sentence tonight", "I grew up without a father: here is what I missed" |

## 10. What I would change, even against your stance

1. **Do not rebuild the site before the offer is proven.** A clean four-page site is enough for the first cohort.
2. **Hide the store and printables.** Zero sales so far, and they blur your message.
3. **Do not launch the Club before a cohort finishes.** An empty club kills trust.
4. **Do not sell on the Stages of Change.** Keep it as your design logic.
5. **Take the pressure off Dec 3.** A small, real first circle beats a fake full one.
6. **Get one clinical backstop.** You are a pastor and a counselor, not an ADHD clinician. Ask a licensed clinician (psychologist, psychiatrist or ADHD-trained therapist) to review your materials and be a referral partner. Say on the site what you are not. This protects men and protects you.
7. **Your story needs to be honest about ADHD.** The site does not say you have it. If you do, say so in your words. If you do not, say plainly that you built this alongside men who do, and let the men's stories (with permission) carry it.
8. **Fix Kit before you promise "arrives in two minutes".** The key is rejected and the plan is locked. Until it works, say "arrives within a day" and make sure the order lands.
9. **Pick one price structure.** Recurring versus one-time. Do not show both.
10. **Stop adding products.** Every new item before the first cohort is a distraction.

## 11. Certifications (free, honestly)

- **No free ADHD coach certification exists.** Free options are awareness-level only: Coursera's introductory ADHD course (audit mode), Reed's ADHD awareness course.
- **Do not buy a credential yet.** Accredited ADHD coach training starts around a few hundred dollars for short programs and runs to about $9,700 for ICF-accredited tracks.
- **Title.** Use "faith-based consistency coach", not "ADHD coach".
- **Your credential.** Add your marriage-counseling certifying body to the site only when you supply the name.
- **Later:** once you have results, consider ICF-accredited coach training. It is optional.
