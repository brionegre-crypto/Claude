Four rules for a promise that survives an ordinary week.

BE THE MAN · STEP 4

“This week” is not a time.

You have a sentence, a short list of goals, and an honest look at what stopped you. Now they have to live somewhere other than your head.

A goal says what. A system says when. These four rules turn one into the other. They’re the rules behind the Weekly Scorecard:

  1. Write the when. Every decision gets a time and a place before the week starts. “Tuesday and Thursday, 9 pm, after the kids are down” is a time. “This week” is not.

  2. Define the miss. Decide in advance what counts as a miss, so a bad week is defined before, not argued about after.

  3. Log it that day. Sunday-night memory is a story, not evidence.

  4. Run the wife test. Could the people in your house see it without you announcing it? If not, it didn’t hold.

Go back to the man whose morning prayer kept dying on late nights. His system doesn’t just say “pray at 6 am.” It says when his evening ends, what counts as a miss, and what he does the morning after one.

Missing once is not failure. The sheet tells you exactly what is.

THE TRIGGER

Your three goals from Step 2, and the obstacle from Step 3.

THE TEST

Every goal has a when, a miss and a wife test written next to it.
