Ten minutes at bedtime. Three questions. No phone.

THE THURSDAY EMAIL · MECHANISM 4

The best meeting of your day.

At the dinner table, kids give one-word answers. “Good.” “Fine.” “Nothing.” Then the lights go off, and suddenly they want to talk about everything. Anyone who has put a child to bed knows this.

That isn’t a stalling tactic. It’s the one time of day when the house is quiet, the screens are off, and they have your full attention in the dark. It’s the best meeting of your day, and most fathers skip it because they’re tired and bedtime has become something to get through.

Turn it into a mechanism. Ten minutes, sitting on the edge of the bed, with three questions:

  1. “What was the best part of today?” Easy to answer. It gets them talking.

  2. “Was anything hard today?” Then wait. The pause is where the real answer comes.

  3. “What can I pray for?” Then pray for it, right there, out loud and short.

Kids too old for tuck-ins? Same questions, in the car, on the drive to practice. Side by side works better than face to face for most teenagers anyway.

THE TIME

Three nights this week. Pick them now.

THE TRIGGER

The light switch. Phone stays in the drawer from the first ten minutes home.

THE TEST

You know one thing about their week you wouldn’t have known otherwise.
