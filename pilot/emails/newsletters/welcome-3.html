Your sentence is the filter. Most goals won’t pass it.

BE THE MAN · STEP 2

Fewer goals than you think.

The last email was the sentence. This one makes it earn its keep.

A goal set before Step 1 is chosen by mood: whatever felt urgent that week. A goal set after it is chosen because the man in your sentence would pursue it.

So put your sentence in front of you and ask one question of every goal: does this move me toward that man, or is it just something I feel I should want?

An example. If your sentence says “home when he is home,” then “phone off from 6 to 8 on weeknights” passes. “Read fifty books this year” might be a fine goal, but unless it serves the sentence, it doesn’t go on this list yet.

Four mistakes men make at this step:

  • Ten goals when three would actually get done

  • Goals so vague you could never tell if you missed one

  • Borrowed goals that didn’t come from your sentence

  • A goal with no idea when in the week it happens

That last one gets fixed later in the framework. For now, get the right goals on paper, and fewer of them than you think.

THE TIME

Sunday evening. Fifteen minutes.

THE TRIGGER

Your Step 1 sentence on the table before you write a single goal.

THE TEST

Three goals or fewer, and you can name the part of the sentence each one serves.
