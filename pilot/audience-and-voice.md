# Be The Man: audience, positioning and voice (set Nov 2026, narrowed to ADHD Nov 19)

The audience is Black Christian men with ADHD. This replaces "Black Christian men" (Oct) and the short-lived "neurodivergent" wording (earlier Nov 19). Everything on the site, in email and on social follows this page.

## 1. Audience statement

**Internal (the one to keep in your head)**

Be The Man serves Black Christian men, ages 25 to 40, who have ADHD (diagnosed, or still asking the question) and who struggle to turn good intentions into follow-through. They love God, love their people and mean what they say. They have more conviction, more ideas and more capacity than their calendar shows. They have been told to try harder and pray harder, and they did both. What they lack is a system built for the brain they actually have.

**Public (the one on the site and in bios)**

For Black Christian men with ADHD, diagnosed or still wondering. You love God, you mean what you say, and you are tired of calling the gap between the two a character flaw. Here the system bends to your brain, not the other way around.

**One line**

Faith-first systems for Black Christian men with ADHD.

## 2. Who he is

- **Life stage.** 25 to 40, in the middle of building: a job or business, maybe a marriage, maybe kids, a calling he can feel. A to-do list bigger than his energy.
- **Wiring.** ADHD. Some have a diagnosis. Many were never assessed, or were labeled "lazy," "scattered," "a behavior problem" or "has so much potential" instead. Some are only now putting words to it. (In a 2023 CDC survey, 55.9% of US adults with ADHD were diagnosed as adults.)
- **Faith.** He prays, believes the Word and takes it seriously. He has heard "pray harder" and "just be disciplined" as the whole answer.
- **The struggle.** He starts strong and stalls. Starting is harder than doing, and once he starts he can go for hours. Time slips ("I'll do it Thursday" and then it's Saturday). He forgets, loses the thread, and drops the plan the day one thing breaks. All or nothing. The Sunday-night shame spiral.
- **What he's tried.** Planners, apps, alarms, accountability partners, a new routine every January. Each worked for two weeks.
- **What he feels.** Ashamed, misunderstood, behind. Afraid of letting down his wife, his kids, his mother, his pastor, God.
- **What he wants.** A Tuesday that goes the way he meant. To keep his word without white-knuckling it. To stop being the man with potential and become the man with results, on his own wiring.
- **What he distrusts.** Hustle culture, clinical jargon, pity, being treated as a project to fix, church platitudes, productivity gurus who don't look like him or live like him.

## 3. Positioning

For Black Christian men, 25 to 40, with ADHD who keep falling between the intention and the doing, Be The Man is a faith-first system built around the way your brain actually works. Unlike productivity advice that runs on willpower, it runs on a written plan, a time and a place, small steps, a bad-day version and brothers who ask.

**What we say that nobody else does:** your brain is not the problem. The system you were handed was built for someone else's brain.

### The six design principles (each maps to something that already exists)

1. **Put it outside your head.** One written sentence (Step 1), a one-page Weekly Scorecard, goals on paper. "Write the vision, make it plain on tablets, so he may run who reads it" (Habakkuk 2:2).
2. **A time, a place, a trigger.** "Thursday, 7:40 PM, phone in the drawer," not "this week." Time is easier to feel when it has an address. (If-then plans: see the statistics table in marketing-scripts.md, section F. The research is in children with ADHD.)
3. **The smallest version that still counts.** Every commitment gets a bad-day version, so a bad week bends the plan instead of ending it. (Thursday email 10 teaches this.)
4. **Define the miss, then count it without shame.** What counts as a miss is decided in advance. "Four out of seven is four out of seven." Missing once is not failure.
5. **Not alone.** An accountability partner, the weekly done-or-missed text, the monthly Club call, the Inner Circle. Working alongside another man makes starting easier for many men with ADHD.
6. **God at the center.** Step 7. The standard is Scripture, not mood, and not how organized you feel.

## 6. Voice

**Sounds like:** a pastor who has sat with a lot of men at a kitchen table. Plain, warm, direct, never clinical, never pitying. Humor is fine. Shame is not.

**Do**
- Name the wall first ("You've started this plan three times"), then the way through.
- Say "your brain" and "your wiring," not "your condition."
- Say "diagnosed or still wondering." Never require a diagnosis.
- Say "bad-day version," "smallest version that still counts," "restart without shame."
- Keep every step small, timed and checkable. Every post ends in one thing to do.
- Cite Scripture for how he is made and for how plans work, not as a whip.

**Don't**
- Don't say cure, fix, heal, treat or manage anyone's ADHD.
- Don't use "disorder," "deficit," "disabled" or "broken" about him. ("Your system is broken" is fine. "You are broken" is never fine.)
- Don't use "lazy," "undisciplined" or "just" except to refute them.
- Don't use "proven." Use "built on" or "linked to."
- Don't use hustle language: grind, hack, crush it, 5 AM club.
- Don't diagnose anyone, in a post, a comment or a call.
- Don't invent testimonials or statistics. Use only the verified statistics in marketing-scripts.md, section F, worded exactly as written there.
- Don't imply a church runs Be The Man.
- Don't use em dashes in site or email copy.

## 7. Claims guardrails

- Brian Greene is a pastor of ten years and a certified marriage counselor. He is not a physician or a psychologist. Be The Man is faith-based coaching and education. It is not medical or mental health treatment and does not replace an assessment or therapy from a licensed clinician. This is stated on the site (home FAQ, About, Built For Your Brain, Terms).
- If a man asks whether he has ADHD, the answer is: "I can't diagnose that. A licensed clinician can. You don't need a diagnosis to start here."
- If someone mentions hopelessness or self-harm: 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) and a human follow-up from Brian.
- Statistics: the two CDC figures are self-reported, from an online survey, and describe all US adults. The if-then-plan studies were in children with ADHD, about age 10. None of the sources report men separately. The site says so. Never present any of them as research on Black men with ADHD or on Be The Man itself.
- Brian's own story stays Brian's. The site does not say he has ADHD. If he does, his story in his words is the strongest thing this brand can have.
- Paid ads: Meta's policy on personal attributes bars ads that assert or imply a person's race, religion or health condition ("Are you a Black Christian man with ADHD?"). Write ads as a situation ("Started the plan again? Stalled again?") and target by age, interests and lookalikes, not by the attributes. Check the current policy before running anything.
