# Marketing scripts for the new audience

Audience: Black Christian men, 25 to 40, with ADHD (diagnosed or still wondering), who struggle with productivity.
Tagline: Built for the brain God gave you. Supporting line: Faith should function.
Rules for every script: no cure or treatment language, no diagnosing, no "proven," no invented stories or numbers (the only statistics we use are the verified ones in section F), no claim that Brian has ADHD, never imply a church runs Be The Man. Scripture is ESV. Brian says "I'm not a doctor" whenever a script gets near a diagnosis.

Format: each short-form script is 40 to 60 seconds spoken. Film vertical for Reels, Shorts and TikTok. Captions on, always: a lot of the audience watches with the sound off and a lot reads faster than it listens.

## A. Twelve short-form scripts

### 1. "Pray harder" is not a plan
**On-screen hook (0-3s):** You've been told to pray harder.
**Spoken:**
You've been told to try harder. Pray harder. Be more disciplined. And you did. You prayed, you tried, you set the alarm, and by Thursday it was gone again.
Hear me: I'm a pastor. I'm not against prayer. But Proverbs 16:9 says the heart of man plans his way, and the Lord establishes his steps. Plans are in the verse. The plan is not a lack of faith.
If you have ADHD, the plan has to be built for that brain: on paper, with a time and a place, and a smaller version for the bad days.
**Scripture on screen:** Proverbs 16:9
**CTA:** Step 1 is free. Twenty minutes, or five minutes at a time. Link in bio.

### 2. Not lazy. Under-systemed.
**Hook:** Nobody told you the truth about "lazy."
**Spoken:**
Somebody called you lazy. Maybe a teacher, maybe a boss, maybe a church mother who meant well. And you believed it, because you do have the energy. You'll work four hours straight on the right thing.
What you don't have is a system built for how you start, how you remember and how you feel time.
That's not lazy. That's under-systemed. And the fix isn't more shame. It's a system that fits.
**CTA:** I built one. Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 3. The Thursday that turned into Saturday
**Hook:** "I'll do it Thursday." Then it was Saturday.
**Spoken:**
"I'll do it Thursday." You meant it. Then you looked up and it was Saturday, and you honestly don't know where Friday went.
Some brains feel time as two things: now and not now. Thursday is "not now," and "not now" has no edges.
So give Thursday an address. Not "this week." Thursday, 7:40 PM, phone in the drawer, the worksheet on the kitchen table. When it has a time and a place, it stops being a feeling and starts being an appointment.
**On-screen:** "This week" is not a time.
**CTA:** One time. One place. Start there. Link in bio.

### 4. The wall is the start
**Hook:** It's not the work that's hard.
**Spoken:**
It's not the work. Once you start, you can go for hours. The wall is the start.
So stop trying to do the whole thing. Do the first five minutes. Open the document. Write one line. Put on your shoes.
Psalm 119:105: your word is a lamp to my feet. A lamp to my feet, not a floodlight on the whole road. One step you can see.
Step 1 works like that. Five qualities, one sentence, and you can do it in pieces.
**CTA:** Start with five minutes. Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 5. The bad-day version
**Hook:** What do you do on the bad day?
**Spoken:**
Every plan you've made works on a good day. What happens on the bad one? You didn't sleep, the kids were up, your head's foggy.
Most plans have one answer: you failed. Mine has a different one: the bad-day version.
Pray for thirty minutes becomes pray for one minute. Work out becomes put on your shoes and walk to the mailbox. It's the smallest version that still counts.
A rough week should bend the plan, not end it.
**CTA:** Every commitment gets a bad-day version. Step 1 is free, link in bio.

### 6. The Sunday-night verdict
**Hook:** Sunday night. Adding up everything you didn't do.
**Spoken:**
Sunday night. You lie there and add up what you didn't do. It feels like a verdict. Lazy. Faithless. Again.
It's not a verdict. It's a number. Four out of seven is four out of seven. It's not "going well" and it's not "I'm a failure." It's four.
Decide what counts as a miss before the week starts, write it on one page, and count it. Count it, don't judge it. Proverbs 24:16: the righteous falls seven times and rises again.
**CTA:** The weekly scorecard is inside the system. Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 7. Write it where you can see it
**Hook:** God told Habakkuk to write it down.
**Spoken:**
Habakkuk 2:2. God doesn't say "hold the vision in your head." He says write the vision, make it plain on tablets, so he may run who reads it.
For a brain that loses the thread, that verse is a strategy. Get it out of your head. Put it where your eyes land: the fridge, the dashboard, the bathroom mirror.
Step 1 is one sentence about the man you're becoming. Write it. Stick it up. Let the wall remember it so you don't have to.
**CTA:** Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 8. Done or missed
**Hook:** The cheapest accountability there is.
**Spoken:**
Here's the cheapest accountability system I know. One brother. One text a week. One word: done, or missed.
No speeches. No guilt trip. Missed is allowed. Hiding it is what breaks it.
Starting is easier when somebody's alongside you. Ecclesiastes 4:10: if they fall, one will lift up his fellow. Pick one man tonight and send him this.
**CTA:** The free 7-week challenge builds it in. Link in bio.

### 9. You've tried every app
**Hook:** The app isn't the problem.
**Spoken:**
You've downloaded the app. Bought the planner. Watched the guy with the morning routine. Each one worked for about two weeks.
It's not that you can't stick with things. It's that most of those tools are built on willpower and a perfect week.
You don't need a better app. You need a plan that survives a bad week: on paper, with a time and a place, a smaller version for the rough days, and a man who'll ask you about it.
**CTA:** Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 10. Do I need a diagnosis?
**Hook:** "Do I have to be diagnosed to start?"
**Spoken:**
I get asked this a lot. Do I need an ADHD diagnosis? No. Diagnosed or still wondering, you can start.
Here's something most men don't know. In a 2023 CDC survey, more than half of US adults with ADHD, 55.9 percent, were diagnosed as adults. A lot of us spent years being called lazy before anybody said the word.
And let me be straight: I'm a pastor and a certified marriage counselor. I'm not a doctor. I can't diagnose you, and this isn't treatment. If you want to find out, a licensed clinician can help.
In the meantime, you don't have to wait to build a system that fits the brain you have.
**On-screen:** 55.9% of US adults with ADHD were diagnosed as adults. (CDC, 2023 survey, self-reported)
**CTA:** Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 11. When you forgot again
**Hook:** She asked you one thing. You forgot. Again.
**Spoken:**
She asked you one thing. You forgot. Again. And now you're sorry, and you mean it, and sorry is not the fix.
Three moves. One: say it plainly, no excuse. "I forgot, and that cost you." Two: repair what you can today. Three: change one thing with a time on it. Not "I'll try harder." A reminder on your phone, a note on the door, a time on the calendar.
An apology is a start. A system is the follow-through.
**CTA:** Free Step 1, link in bio.

### 12. Fearfully and wonderfully made
**Hook:** Your wiring is not a mistake.
**Spoken:**
Psalm 139:14. I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made.
Not "fearfully and wonderfully made, except the part of you that loses your keys." Made. On purpose. Including the part that hyperfocuses, the part that notices what nobody else notices, the part that can't sit still through a long meeting.
You don't have to change how you're made. You have to build a system that fits it. 1 Corinthians 12:18: God arranged the members in the body, each one, as he chose.
**CTA:** Built for the brain God gave you. Free Step 1, link in bio.

## F. The statistics we can use (and what each one does and does not say)

Only these. Each was read at the source on Nov 19, 2026. Do not add a number to a script, post, ad or email without a source.

| Statistic | Source | Say it like this | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.0% of US adults (about 15.5 million) reported a current ADHD diagnosis in 2023 | CDC, MMWR, Oct 10, 2024, 73(40):890-895, https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7340a1.htm | "About 6 percent of US adults, roughly 15.5 million people, report a current ADHD diagnosis (CDC, 2023)." | That it is a clinical prevalence rate. It is self-reported, from an online panel survey. |
| 55.9% of adults with a current ADHD diagnosis were diagnosed as adults (44.1% as children) | Same | "More than half of adults with ADHD were diagnosed as adults (CDC, 2023 survey)." | Any claim by sex or race. The report does not give those breakdowns. |
| If-then plans helped children with ADHD: fewer shifting errors and better working-memory math (Study 1 and 2); better delay of gratification (45 inpatient children, then 47 outpatient children with ADHD plus 40 without) | Gawrilow, Gollwitzer & Oettingen (2011), Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology 30(6):616-646; and Cognitive Therapy and Research 35:442-455 | "In two 2011 studies, children with ADHD who made if-then plans did better on tasks needing attention-shifting, working memory and waiting." | That it was tested in adults or in men. It was children, about age 10. Never say "proven." Be The Man itself has not been studied. |

**Not used, on purpose**
- A stat that Black men are under-diagnosed with ADHD. The evidence is mixed (one 2024 health-records study found Black patients less likely to be diagnosed; a separate meta-analysis found higher prevalence). The CDC survey above has only 30 Black adults with ADHD and gives no significance test.
- The 91% versus 38% figure (written plans, general population) and the 66-day habit figure. They are not about ADHD. They are off the public pages. The Club's month-3 and month-4 member lessons still quote a few general studies.
- Anything about "men with ADHD" specifically. The sources above do not report men separately.
