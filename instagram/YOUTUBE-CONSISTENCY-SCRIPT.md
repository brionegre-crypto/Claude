## 0:00 HOOK (about 40 seconds)

## 0:40 THE ANSWER IN ONE SENTENCE (about 45 seconds)

Here it is, up front. You don't stay consistent because you're motivated. You stay consistent because you built something that doesn't need you to be motivated.

Consistency isn't a personality trait. It's a design problem. Men who look disciplined have mostly just built a setup where the right thing is the easy thing, and they've stopped relying on how they feel.

[ON SCREEN: "Consistency is a design problem."]

## 1:25 WHY WILLPOWER FAILS (about 1:45)

Think about what you're actually doing on Sunday night. You're making a plan while you're rested, fresh and hopeful. And then you're asking Wednesday-you, who's tired, behind at work and short on sleep, to carry it out.

Researchers who study habits describe this as a motivation wave. It spikes when you start something new, and then it drops. It's not a flaw in you. It's how motivation works. A plan that depends on it is a plan that quits when the wave does.

[YOUR STORY: one short, specific Wednesday. A time you meant it on Sunday and lost it midweek. Two or three sentences, with a real detail like what you skipped and what you told yourself. If you don't have one: "I'd say 'I'll pray tomorrow morning.' Tomorrow came and I hadn't decided when. I didn't fail at prayer. I never gave it a time."]

And people tell men, "just try harder." But trying harder is just more of the thing that already failed. A man who white-knuckles his way through week one has no plan for week three.

How long does it really take? You've heard 21 days. A well-known study out of University College London tracked people forming a new habit and found it took about 66 days on average, and anywhere from 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the habit. [VERIFY before filming: Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology.] Two months, on average. Which means "I'll just push through for a few weeks" was never enough.

## 3:10 THE THREE PARTS OF A SYSTEM THAT HOLDS (about 2:30)

So what holds? Not conviction. A system. And a system has three parts.

[ON SCREEN: "A time. A trigger. A way to tell if it held."]

**One: a time.** Not "in the morning." Not "when I get a chance." A time. "Six fifteen, before the kids are up." A goal says what. A system says when. If it doesn't have a when, it's not a plan. It's a wish.

**Two: a trigger.** The thing that happens right before, so you don't have to remember. After the coffee. After I park the car. After the kids go down. You stop asking yourself "should I?" because the trigger decides. The decision is already made.

**Three: a way to tell whether it held.** This is the part most men skip. If you can't see whether it held, you can't fix it, and you're back to guessing, which means back to feelings. One page. A box you can check. Did it happen, yes or no. At the end of the week, you look. Not to beat yourself up. To see.

[SHOW ON SCREEN: your Weekly Scorecard. In your own words: what it tracks and how a man uses it on Sunday. Keep it to 30 to 45 seconds. I can't see its contents, so describe what it actually is.]

Notice what all three have in common. None of them need you to feel like it. A time, a trigger and a page that tells the truth. That's the system.

## 5:40 TWO RULES THAT KEEP IT ALIVE (about 1:30)

Two more rules, because systems get tested.

**Make it small enough that you can't fail.** Don't plan an hour of Bible study. Plan five minutes. Five minutes you'll actually do beats an hour you'll skip. You can always do more. You can't make up for a plan you've quit.

**Never miss twice.** You're going to miss. A sick kid, a hard week. The rule isn't "never miss." It's that one miss is a day, and two misses is a new habit, the habit of quitting. So after a miss, the only job is to get back on the next day. Not to restart Monday. The next day.

That's it. Small enough, and never miss twice.

## 7:10 WHAT SCRIPTURE SAYS (about 1 minute)

God isn't surprised by any of this, and he doesn't ask you to be impressive. He asks you to be faithful.

Luke 16:10: "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much." Faithful in the little. That's your five minutes.

Proverbs 24:16: "For the righteous falls seven times and rises again." The righteous man isn't the man who never falls. He's the one who gets back up. Never miss twice is just that, with a time attached.

And Galatians 6:9: "Let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up." Not if we feel great. If we do not give up.
