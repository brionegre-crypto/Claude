## 0:40 THE IDEA: A DECISION WITH A TIME (about 35 seconds)

A decision isn't "be more loving." That's a wish. A decision is something you'll do, with a time on it. "Tuesday at 8, after the kids are down, I'll ___."

Time turns a good intention into something you can actually do. So here are seven examples.

## 1:15 THE SEVEN (about 2:00)

[ON SCREEN: each one appears as you say it]

One. Pray with her. Three nights a week, after the kids are down, one minute. Hold her hand. Ask what she needs prayer for, and pray for that.

Two. Ask the weekly question. Sunday at eight: "What's one thing I could do this week that would help you?" Then listen. Don't defend yourself.

Three. Phone down. Weeknights at six-thirty, it goes in the drawer until the kids are in bed. Your phone isn't more important than your family. Show it.

Four. Do one thing she doesn't have to ask for. Wednesday after dinner, you take the kitchen. Dishes, counters, done. Without a reminder.

Five. Say something specific. Tuesday and Thursday at lunch, text her one thing you noticed. Not "love you." "I saw how you handled that with the kids, and I'm grateful."

Six. Be home when you're home. After seven, no work email. When you walk in, you're in.

Seven. Lead one small thing with the kids. Saturday at nine, one thing together. Or a short bedtime prayer.

[YOUR STORY (optional): one sentence about the one of these that changed something in your own marriage. A real detail.]

## 3:15 HOW TO START (about 50 seconds)

Now. Do not try all seven this week. That's how you quit by Wednesday.

Pick three. Write each one with its time. Do them for a week. Next week, add one.

And when you miss one, and you will, same rule. Next day, same time. No catching up, and no speech about how you failed. Just do the small thing again.

Keep a checkmark for each one. Seeing it on paper changes what you do.
