## 0:45 WHY BLIND SPOTS HAVE POWER (about 1:45)

You know the blind spot in your car. The patch beside you that your mirrors don't show. You check your mirrors, you feel completely safe, and you drift into the lane anyway.

Notice what's dangerous about it. It's not that you're careless. It's that you *feel* fine. A blind spot never feels like a blind spot. Nothing is telling you something's missing, so you don't go looking.

It's the same inside a man.

If you knew you were short with your kids when you're tired, you'd fix it. If you knew your wife has stopped telling you things because the last three times it didn't go well, you'd do something. You can only fix what you can see.

So the thing running your life is never the problem you're working on. You're working on the problems you can see. The one costing you the most is in the part you can't.

[ON SCREEN: "You can't fix what you can't see."]

And here's the part that stings. Most of us believe we're the exception. The researcher Tasha Eurich studied self-awareness and found that the large majority of people believe they're self-aware, while only a small fraction actually are. [VERIFY the exact figures before filming: her 2018 Harvard Business Review work reports roughly 95% think they are and about 10 to 15% are.]

In other words, the confident feeling of knowing yourself is not evidence that you do.

## 2:30 THE THREE PLACES THEY HIDE (about 2 minutes)

For a Christian man, blind spots tend to hide in three places. Keep these in mind and ask yourself honestly as we go.

**One: how you come across.** You think you're being direct. They hear harsh. You think you're being quiet and steady. They hear distant. The words you meant and the words that landed are not the same words.

**Two: what you avoid.** Every man has a conversation, a habit, a bill or a prayer he quietly steps around. You don't call it avoiding. You call it being busy. You can't see it because you've renamed it.

**Three: what you've gotten used to.** The phone in your hand at dinner. The tone you take when you're stressed. You stopped noticing years ago. Your kids didn't.

[YOUR STORY: one short, specific moment when someone showed you something about yourself you honestly hadn't seen. Keep it to two or three sentences. A real moment beats any explanation. If you don't have one, use: "A man I respect once told me X. I argued for about ten seconds. Then I realized he was right."]

Here's the pattern: none of these three feel like sin or failure from the inside. They feel normal. That's exactly why a man can love God, love his family, work hard, and still be stuck.

## 4:30 WHAT SCRIPTURE SAYS (about 1:30)

Scripture does not treat this as a small thing.

Jesus, in Matthew 7: "Why do you see the speck that is in your brother's eye, but do not notice the log that is in your own eye?" Notice he doesn't say "you ignored the log." He says you *do not notice* it. That's a blind spot, named two thousand years ago.

Proverbs 12:15: "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but a wise man listens to advice." The danger isn't that the path looks wrong. It's that it looks right.

And look at what David prays in Psalm 139: "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts! And see if there be any grievous way in me." He doesn't say "I've examined myself and I'm fine." He asks God to show him what he can't see.

[ON SCREEN: Psalm 139:23-24]

That is the posture. Not shame. Not guilt. Just an honest request: show me.

## 6:00 THE ONLY FIX THAT WORKS (about 1:30)

So how does a man see what he can't see?

Not by trying harder. You can't use the eyes that have the blind spot to find the blind spot. That's the whole definition.

You need a mirror from outside you. Scripture says the same: "Faithful are the wounds of a friend" in Proverbs 27:6, and "Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another" in Proverbs 27:17.

And the research agrees on the shape of it. Written plans with a time attached work. Tracking progress on paper works. And a large review of 138 experiments found that when people report their progress to other people, they follow through more. [Source on your page: Harkin et al., 2016. Say "a review of 138 experiments" and don't claim a number beyond that.]

Put those together and you get a very specific recipe: *write it down, track it, and let other men see it.*

Three things. A number on paper that doesn't flatter you. A few men who will tell you the truth. And a regular rhythm, so it isn't one conversation and then back to normal.

## Notes for filming

- **The ask appears three times on purpose:** a soft mention in the hook ("stay for the next nine minutes"), the specific offer at 7:30 (what, when, what's included), and the final ask at 9:00 (price, link, fallback). Don't add a fourth; it turns into a pitch.
- **Say "pinned comment" and "first link"** out loud so the viewer knows where to click.
- **Cut anything that drags.** If the take runs over 10 minutes, trim the three-places section, not the offer.
- **Keep the fallback.** Not every viewer buys a group. Step 1 free keeps them in your email list, where the Thursday emails convert later.
- **Don't promise a result.** "You leave with a plan for January" and "a regular rhythm" are honest. "You'll change" is not.
- **Verify the Eurich figures** (flagged in the 0:45 section) and read the Harkin line against your source before filming.
