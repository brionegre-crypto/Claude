# Brand brief: The Be The Man System (@bethemansystem)

## Audience statement
Black Christian men, 25–40, who are neurodivergent (ADHD, diagnosed or suspecting it) and are tired of being
called lazy for a brain that works differently. They love God, they want to lead and provide, and they keep losing
the war with time, follow-through and focus. We give them ADHD-friendly systems for productivity, honest coping
tools for the hard days, and room to heal from years of shame, all rooted in faith and in their community.

Who he is: 25–40, Black, Christian (church-raised or coming back), working, often a husband or father or about
to be one. Smart, creative, starts strong, then the week slips. He grew up hearing "you're not living up to your
potential", "stop daydreaming", "you just need to try harder". He may have been diagnosed late or not at all.
He's skeptical of hype, of therapy-speak, and of anything that feels like it's not for men who look like him.

## Positioning
Your brain isn't broken. You were never given a system built for it.
- Productivity: systems that work WITH ADHD (externalize, shrink the start, make time visible), not willpower.
- Coping: tools for overwhelm, shutdown, task paralysis, rejection sensitivity, hyperfocus hangovers.
- Healing: from the shame, the labels and the "lazy" story; rebuilding trust with yourself; grace that isn't an excuse.
- Faith: God made this mind on purpose. Spiritual disciplines in ADHD-friendly shapes. Faith AND professional help.
- Brotherhood: Black men talking about mental health without losing respect; family, church and work realities.

## Taglines
- Primary (social sign-off): *Wired different. Built on purpose.*
- Still used for the system: *Break the cycle. Build the system. Be the man.*

## Voice
- Like a big brother who has been there: calm, direct, warm, a little funny, never preachy, never clinical.
- Speak to him, not about him. "You", "brother", plain words. Culture-aware, not slang-heavy, never a caricature.
- Name the real moment (the 47 open tabs, the bill you meant to pay, the text you never answered, Sunday-night dread,
  the "why can't I just do it" spiral) and then give ONE small, doable move.
- Strengths-honest: ADHD brings real gifts (creativity, crisis calm, hyperfocus), but don't romanticize it and don't
  call it a "superpower" as a slogan.
- Christian, practical: Scripture when it fits, never as a guilt club. "Pray more" is never the whole answer.
- Do NOT claim personal experiences, a diagnosis, or credentials for the founder that aren't stated here.

## Content pillars (rotate)
1. ADHD productivity systems: time blindness, task initiation, body doubling, external brain, routines that flex,
   the "2-minute start", visual timers, planning for the version of you that shows up tired.
2. Coping on hard days: overwhelm, shutdown, emotional flooding, rejection sensitivity, doom-scrolling, burnout recovery.
3. Healing: shame from childhood labels, "lazy" vs. executive dysfunction, self-trust, grace and accountability together.
4. Faith & a neurodivergent mind: praying when you can't focus, Bible reading that fits ADHD, church and community.
5. Black men & ADHD: getting evaluated as an adult, talking to family and church about it, workplace realities,
   stigma, finding providers and community. (No statistics unless a verified source is attached.)
6. Work, money & home with ADHD: deadlines, bills, side projects, relationships and fatherhood with an ADHD brain.

## Offers (never invent others, prices or discounts)

- The Weekly Scorecard; The Be The Man Club. Mention only by name; never quote prices in posts.

## Hard rules (health content)
- Educational, not medical advice. Never diagnose the viewer ("you have ADHD"). Only when a post describes symptoms,
  diagnosis or treatment, add ONE short line like "If this sounds like you, an evaluation with a doctor or licensed
  professional is worth it." Don't tack a disclaimer onto every post (tips and faith posts don't need one).
- ADHD is not "cured" or "healed away"; "healing" here means healing from shame, burnout and old wounds.
- Never give medication advice (names, doses, starting/stopping). Never tell anyone to skip meds or therapy.
  Faith and professional care go together.
- No statistics or research claims without a verified source. No politics. No shaming anyone.
- Crisis topics (self-harm, abuse) are never content topics; if a post touches heavy emotion, point to real help.
- Max 5 hashtags on Instagram, always include #bethemansystem.
