# How far Be The Man can go, and what to add (2026-10-05)

Status: research and recommendations. Nothing here has been built.

## The market

- About 8.2 million Black fathers in the U.S., and about 5.2 million Black married-couple families (Census figures via BET/Statista).
- Black Protestants attend at the highest rate of any U.S. religious group: 66% take part at least monthly (Pew).
- But fewer Black men are in the pews, especially younger men. Studies put 70–80% of Black men outside regular attendance, and 28% of Black Gen Z and 33% of Black millennials are unaffiliated (Pew, Spokesman-Recorder, CT). That gap is the opening: men who still believe but aren't in a men's group.
- Very few paid programs speak directly to Black Christian husbands and fathers. The big names (33 The Series/Men's Fraternity, Tony Evans' Kingdom Men, Iron Sharpens Iron) are church-delivered and either general-market or not systems-based. Online brotherhoods (Powerful Christian Men, Brotherhood Forged, IMPACT Players, Masculine Revival) are mostly general-market.

## What realistic success looks like

Benchmarks:
- Free email list → paid membership: typically 1–3% for small lists, 2–5% for larger, engaged ones (Nieman Lab, beehiiv data).
- Paid communities lose 25–55% of new members in the first 60 days; monthly churn around 8% means replacing the whole base yearly. Engagement, not price, drives churn.

A worked example (an illustration, not a forecast):

| Item | Assumption | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Club | 100 members at $19 | $1,900 |
| Couples counseling | 2 couples a month at $300 | $600 |
| 6-week group | 4 seats at $297 every two months | ~$600 |
| Store and Library | ~10 sales a month, average ~$40 | ~$400 |
| **Total** | | **~$3,500** |

To hold 100 Club members at 2% conversion and 8% monthly churn, you need about 8 new members a month. That takes about 400 new email subscribers a month, and a list of roughly 5,000 in total. List growth is the number that decides everything else.

## What others do that Be The Man doesn't yet

1. **Proof from real people.** Every competitor leads with stories. The site has none yet. Collect them from the founding group and the first couples, with written permission. Never invent or composite them.
2. **A show (podcast or weekly YouTube).** Almost every brand in this space has one. You already have a YouTube channel. A 20-minute weekly episode, cut into shorts, is the cheapest discovery engine and it feeds the email list.
3. **A free challenge with a start date.** RISE (Catholic) charges $32 for a 30-day challenge done with a "brother". A free or low-cost "7 Tuesdays" challenge, starting together on a set date and done with a partner, fills the list faster than an always-available worksheet. Run one every quarter.
4. **Selling to churches, not only men.** 33 The Series sells a leader kit for about $75 plus a guide for each man at about $16. A "Be The Man for your men's ministry" kit (leader guide, 7 sessions, worksheets, licence for up to 25 men) at $149–$299 lets one pastor bring 10–30 men at once. This is the biggest gap. Black churches have men's ministries looking for material.
5. **Live gatherings.** Iron Sharpens Iron runs one-day conferences at $44–$79 a head. A half-day "Be The Man" workshop in Huntsville, then in partner churches, builds trust and the list.
6. **A free community front porch.** Several brands run a free Facebook group or Discord where men taste the brotherhood before paying. Today the brotherhood is behind the paywall.
7. **Referral rewards.** "Bring a brother: you both get a month free." Accountability is the pitch, so the referral is natural.
8. **Content Google can find.** A short article for each step and each tool ("how to lead family devotions when you've never done it", "premarital counseling Huntsville"). Canva sites are weak at this; a free Substack you already have can carry it.
9. **Seasonal campaigns.** New Year, Father's Day, Valentine's (couples), back to school. Competitors plan their year around these.

## What Be The Man does that others don't (keep it)

- **Named for Black Christian men.** Most programs say "men". Say who it's for in every bio, title and ad.
- **Pastor and certified marriage counselor in one.** You can serve the man and then the couple. Others do one or the other.
- **A system with times and failure conditions,** not motivation. That's the brand promise; keep it in every piece.
- **Sources cited.** Competitors claim "proven". Keep "built on" with the citation.
- **Low price.** $19 a month is under most paid brotherhoods. Don't drop lower; add value instead.

## Order to do it in

1. Now: publish the site, set up Search Console and Google Business, and collect permission-based stories from the founding group.
2. Next 30 days: the first free "7 Tuesdays" challenge with a start date, promoted on YouTube and Instagram.
3. Next 60 days: the church men's-ministry kit, offered first to five pastors you know.
4. Next 90 days: a weekly show and one in-person workshop in Huntsville.
