Step 1 of 4 · The Calling · Be The Man

Who has God called you to be?

Not what you want to do. Who God is calling you to be, in the roles you already carry. Most men skip this and go straight to goals. Then the week gets hard, there's nothing underneath the goal, and it quietly goes.

"Write the vision; make it plain on tablets, so he may run who reads it." Habakkuk 2:2

  • 20 minutes, or 5 at a time
  • A pen and a Bible, if you print it
  • Somewhere quiet, after the house goes down

Built for ADHD brains. Each numbered move is its own stopping point. Do one, close the page, come back later. One move done is a win.

Type straight into this page. It saves on this device as you go, so you can stop after any move. Or print it and write by hand.

1

List your roles. about 3 min

Write the roles you carry right now, up to five. Start with the one under all the others: a son of God.

Stuck? TapCircle any that fit.

2

Ask what God expects of you in each. about 7 min

Use Scripture, not feelings. One line per role. If you don't know a verse, write what you know God asks of a man in that role, and look it up later.

For example: Husband: "Love her as Christ loved the church" (Ephesians 5:25). Worker: "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord" (Colossians 3:23).

3

Make it a Tuesday. about 5 min

For each role, write what faithful looks like on a normal Tuesday, and the smallest version that still counts on a bad day. If you can't picture it on a specific day, make it smaller.

For example: Husband on a Tuesday: "Phone in the drawer from 7:40 to 9, and I ask about her day." On a bad day, at least: "Five minutes of real attention when I walk in."

4

Write the sentence. about 5 min

One sentence that names who God has called you to be, and why it matters. Build it from what you wrote above. Every part should be something you could check on a given day.

Aim for this"I am a man who is home when he is home, keeps his word to his wife, and lets his kids see him pray, so that my house knows what faithful looks like."
Not this"I want to be a better husband and father." True, and nobody could tell on Tuesday whether it held.

Check it before you call it done

Put it where you'll see it

Out of sight is out of mind, so put the sentence where your eyes already go. Pick one place and one time you'll read it every day.

Lock screen in 30 seconds: write the sentence on paper or tap Copy my sentence, take a photo, and set it as your lock screen.

When you miss a day

Missing isn't failing. It's information. Read your sentence, do one bad-day version, and keep going. "The righteous falls seven times and rises again" (Proverbs 24:16).

What comes next

Step 2 is The Commitments. Your sentence becomes no more than three commitments you can check each week. Step 3, The Floor, gives each one a time, a place and a bad-day version. Step 4, The Ledger, is the weekly scorecard you report to other men. The Founding Cohort builds Steps 2 to 4 with you, live, over six weeks.
