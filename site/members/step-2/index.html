Step 2: what are your goals?

Download the Step 2 worksheet (PDF)

Now that you know who you want to be, you can decide what you are going after. The order matters. A goal set before Step 1 is chosen by mood: whatever felt urgent that week. A goal set after Step 1 is chosen because the man in your sentence would pursue it.

Use your Step 1 sentence as the filter

Keep your Step 1 sentence in front of you while you work the worksheet. For every goal you write down, ask one question: does this move me toward the man in that sentence, or is it just something I feel I should want?

Say your sentence includes "home when he is home". A goal to be off the phone from 6 to 8 pm on weeknights passes the filter. A goal to read fifty books this year might be good, but unless it connects to the sentence, it does not belong on this page yet.

Common mistakes at this step

  • Writing ten goals when three would actually get done
  • Keeping goals so vague you could never tell if you missed one
  • Copying someone else's goals instead of deriving them from your sentence
  • Adding a goal with no idea when in the week it would happen

The last mistake is the one Steps 3 to 6 are built to fix. A goal says what. A system says when. For now, just get the right goals on paper, and fewer of them than you think.

Before Step 3: you should be able to point at each goal and say which part of your Step 1 sentence it serves.
