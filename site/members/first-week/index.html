Your first week in the club

Your first seven days have one job: to put a system in place before the enthusiasm wears off. Three things, in this order. None of them takes more than thirty minutes.

Day 1 or 2: do Step 1

Open Step 1: What type of man do you want to be? in the framework section. Print it. Sit somewhere without your phone, with a pen, for twenty minutes.

You will list the attributes of that man, write what each one looks like on an ordinary Tuesday, and describe how the people in your house would know without you telling them. It ends in one sentence that says who he is.

If you have already done Step 1 from the free download, do it again anyway. A second pass, a few weeks later, is almost always sharper than the first.

Day 3 or 4: set up the Weekly Scorecard

Go to Month 1: the Weekly Scorecard and Forty Cards under Your monthly tools and download it. Pick the decisions for your first week from your Step 1 sentence, and for each one write down three things before the week starts:

  • When it happens. "This week" is not a time. "Tuesday and Thursday, 9 pm, after the kids are down" is.
  • What counts as a miss. Decide it now, so a bad week is defined before, not argued about after.
  • How you would know it held. Could your wife see it without you announcing it? If not, it did not hold.

Then log it on the day it happens. Sunday-night memory is a story, not evidence.

Day 7: your first Sunday review

Block fifteen minutes on Sunday. Put your Step 1 sentence next to your scorecard and answer three questions honestly:

  1. Which decisions held, and which did not?
  2. For each miss, what was the trigger? A time that was never realistic, a situation you did not plan for, or simply no decision at all?
  3. What is the one change you will make to next week's scorecard?

Missing once is not failure. The sheet tells you exactly what is: a miss you never name.

Check yourself at the end of the week

  • You have a one-sentence statement of the man you want to be
  • Your scorecard has a when, a miss and a test for every decision
  • You logged each day on the day, not from memory
  • You named at least one miss and one change for next week

Then move to Step 2 and keep one step per week from here on. Say your Step 1 sentence out loud to one other man this week. Men who say it in front of other men tend to keep it.
