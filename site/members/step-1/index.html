Step 1: what type of man do you want to be?

Download the Step 1 worksheet (PDF)

Most men start with a goal: lose the weight, fix the temper, pray every morning. The goal feels like progress, but it has nothing underneath it. When the week gets hard, there is no reason to keep going that is bigger than the goal itself.

Step 1 comes before the goal. It asks one question, and the answer becomes the standard every later step is measured against.

How to work this step

Print the worksheet. Give it twenty minutes with a pen, not your phone. Work through it in this order:

  1. Name the attributes. Write down the qualities of the man you want to be. Patient. Present. Faithful. Steady under pressure. Use your own words.
  2. Make each one ordinary. For every attribute, write what it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday. "Patient" becomes "I do not raise my voice when homework runs late."
  3. Apply the house test. Write how the people you live with would know, without you telling them.
  4. Write the sentence. Finish with one sentence that says who he is.

What a finished sentence looks like

Here is an example of the kind of sentence this step is aiming for: "I am a man who is home when he is home, keeps his word to his wife, and lets his kids see him pray." Each part can be checked on a given day, and each part would be visible to someone else.

Compare it with "I want to be a better husband and father." That is true, but nobody could tell on Tuesday whether it held.

Before you move to Step 2

  • Every attribute has an ordinary-Tuesday version
  • Someone in your house could notice each one without being told
  • Your sentence fits on one line and you could say it from memory
  • You have written it somewhere you will see it every Sunday

Bring your sentence to the next monthly call, or send it to your accountability partner. Hearing how other men answered the same question is one of the fastest ways to sharpen your own.
