Leader guide

Session 5: check the four pillars

What every man leaves with: his weakest pillar named, and one adjustment to his system based on last week's actual number.

Hand out: the Step 5 worksheet, "The four pillars," and a fresh Scorecard.

Read aloud: Joshua 24:15, and Ephesians 6:4.

Report (15 minutes)

This is the first real number. Each man reads his system and his count: "Four nights planned, two done." No explaining yet. Write every number down.

When the round is done, say what the numbers show without naming anyone: "Most of us kept about half. That's normal for week one of a system. Now we use it."

The teaching

Every part of a man's life lands in one of four places. Most men are strong in one and absent in another. Tonight each man checks all four.

Cycles: what you inherited and never agreed to. Systems: doing the right thing on a day you don't feel like it. Fatherhood: giving your children what you weren't given. Discipline: keeping going when nobody is watching.

Joshua decides for his whole house, out loud. Paul tells fathers not to provoke their children but to bring them up. Both are decisions made on purpose, not left to mood. The worksheet asks each man to rate himself in each pillar and pick the weakest one.

Use the number to adjust, not to judge

After the worksheet, each man looks at his own number and makes one change. Only one. The rules for a good change:

  • If he kept most days, keep the system the same for another week
  • If he missed at the same moment every time, move the time, not the goal
  • If he missed because the action was too big, make it smaller
  • If his weakest pillar isn't covered by any of his three goals, note it for session 7

A hypothetical example: a man planned prayer with his kids at 8:45 on four nights and did it once. Every miss was because the youngest was already asleep. He moves it to 8:00, right after baths. Same goal, new time. That's an adjustment. "I'll try harder" is not.

Discussion questions

  • Which pillar did your father leave you strongest in, and which weakest?
  • What would it sound like for you to say Joshua 24:15 in your own house?
  • What did your number tell you that you didn't want to hear?

Commit and pray

Each man tells his partner his weakest pillar and his one adjustment, and writes the adjusted system on his new Scorecard. Same commitment as last week: fill it in every night, text the number once a week.

Leader check

  • Every man reported a number, not a feeling
  • Every man made one adjustment, or chose on purpose to keep it the same
  • Nobody was shamed for a low number, including by himself
