Leader guide

Session 1: decide who you are building

What every man leaves with: one written sentence describing the man he is becoming, read aloud to his partner.

Hand out: the Step 1 worksheet, "What type of man do you want to be?"

Read aloud: Ephesians 4:22 to 24.

Night one has no report, because nobody has a number yet. Use the first 15 minutes for introductions instead: each man gives his name and answers one question, "What promise have you made more than twice?" Go first, and be honest. The room will match the level you set.

The teaching, in your own words

Most men start with goals: lose twenty pounds, pray every morning, stop yelling. That's why the goals don't hold. A goal with no man behind it is a wish with a deadline. When the goal gets hard, there's nothing underneath it to hold it up.

So we start one step earlier. Before we decide what to do, we decide who is doing it. Paul tells us to put off the old self and put on the new one. That isn't a mood. It's a decision about identity, and it comes first.

Tonight each of you writes five attributes of the man you want to be, what each one looks like on an ordinary Tuesday, and how the people in your house would know it without you telling them. Then one sentence that holds all of it. That sentence becomes the standard every other step gets checked against.

Discussion questions (pick two or three)

  • Who is a man you've watched up close who was the same at home as he was in public?
  • Which of your promises was really about how you look, not who you are?
  • What would your children say you're like on a weeknight?
  • Where does Ephesians 4 say the change happens, and why does that order matter?

What a finished sentence looks like

Too vague: "I want to be a better husband and father." Nobody could tell whether that happened.

Usable: "I am a man who is home when he's home, keeps his word to his wife, and prays with his children before they sleep." Someone in his house could watch for each part of that.

During the writing time, walk the room. If a man's sentence could describe anyone, ask him: "How would your wife know?" Then let him rewrite it.

Commit and pray

Pair the men using the partner agreement from the setup lesson. Each man reads his sentence to his partner. The commitment for the week is simple: tape the sentence somewhere you'll see it every morning, and text your partner "done" when it's up.

Partners pray for each other by name. Close on time.

Leader check after the session

  • Every man has a sentence written in his own hand
  • Every man has a partner and the partner's number
  • You finished within five minutes of your end time
  • You know which men struggled to write, so you can call them this week

Session 2 starts with each man reading his sentence again, so it needs to be in his pocket next week.
