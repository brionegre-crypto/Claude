Leader guide

Session 2: cut to three goals

What every man leaves with: exactly three goals, each tied to a word in his sentence from session 1.

Hand out: the Step 2 worksheet, "What are your goals?"

Read aloud: Philippians 3:13 and 14.

Report (15 minutes)

Go around the table. Each man reads his sentence and says one thing: is it taped up, yes or no? Don't let anyone explain a no yet. You're teaching the room that a report is a fact, not a story. Thank each man and move on.

The teaching

Most men are carrying ten goals: the gym, the budget, the temper, the phone, the prayer life, the side business, the marriage, the kids' homework. Ten goals means none of them gets a time. Paul says "one thing I do." Not ten.

Tonight each man lists every goal he's carrying, then holds each one up against his sentence and asks: does this make me more like that man? He keeps three and crosses out the rest. Not forever, just for these seven weeks.

Here's the hard part to say out loud: if a goal doesn't answer to your sentence, it's somebody else's goal. Your father's, your pastor's, the man on your phone. Some good goals get crossed out tonight, and that's the point.

Discussion questions

  • Which goal have you started the most times?
  • Which of your goals did someone else hand you?
  • What did Paul have to leave behind to say "one thing I do"?
  • What will it cost you to cross out the goal you most want to keep?

Watch for this during the writing

Some men will keep three goals that are really one goal said three ways, like "pray more," "read my Bible" and "grow spiritually." That's fine if it's honest, but ask them to merge it into one and pick two more. Others will try to sneak in a fourth. Hold the line at three. The limit is the lesson.

A hypothetical example: a man starts with nine goals. His sentence says he "is home when he's home." He keeps "phone away during dinner and bedtime," "one date night a month" and "coach my son's practice twice a week," and crosses out the promotion, the half-marathon and the garage. He looks unhappy. Tell him that's what it looks like when a goal finally has room to work.

Commit and pray

Each man reads his three goals to his partner. The commitment for the week: tell one person at home which three you kept, and why. Text your partner "done" or "missed" once a week, on the night you agreed.

Partners pray over each other's three. Close on time.

Leader check

  • Every man has three goals, no more
  • Each goal connects to a word in his sentence
  • The report stayed under 15 minutes
  • Men who said "no" in the report got a call from you this week

Session 3 asks what has stopped these three goals before, so the list needs to come back next week.
