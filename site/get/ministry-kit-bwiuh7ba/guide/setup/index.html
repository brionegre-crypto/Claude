Leader guide

Set up your group and run a session

By the end of this lesson you will have a date, a room, a list of men and a printed stack of worksheets, and you will know exactly what happens in each of the 75 minutes of a session. Every session in this guide uses the same shape, so once you've run one, you can run all seven.

You don't need to be a teacher, a counselor or the most disciplined man in the room. Your job is to keep time, ask the questions, and make sure every man leaves with one written commitment and a partner who will ask about it.

Who to invite

The kit works best with 4 to 12 men. Below four, one absence kills the room. Above twelve, men stop talking and start listening, and this material only works if they talk. If you have more than twelve, run two groups on different nights.

Invite men who keep making the same promise: to be more present, to pray with their wives, to put the phone down, to be patient with their children. You are not looking for men who have it together. You are looking for men who are tired of meaning it and changing nothing.

Ask personally. A bulletin announcement gets you three men. A conversation in the parking lot gets you the right ten. Say it plainly: "Seven weeks, one night a week, 75 minutes. We each leave with a plan that has a time on it. Will you come?"

What to print before night one

  • One copy of each of the seven step worksheets per man, from your download page
  • One Weekly Scorecard per man per week, or print a stack of 8 each
  • A sign-in sheet with each man's name, phone number and email
  • Two pens per man, because someone always forgets
  • A copy of the partner agreement in this lesson, one per pair

Your licence covers printing for every man in your group, for as long as your ministry runs it. Hand out each worksheet on the night it's used, not all at once, so nobody reads ahead and skips the work.

The shape of every session

Each session plan in the next module follows this 75-minute outline. The times matter. A session that runs long teaches the men that the plan is optional, which is the opposite of what this material teaches.

  1. Open, 5 minutes. Welcome, a short prayer, phones face down on the table.
  2. Report, 15 minutes. Each man says his number from the week: "I said Monday and Thursday at 7:40. I did it once out of two." Numbers only, no stories yet. From session 2 onward this is the most important part of the night.
  3. Teach, 15 minutes. You read or summarise the teaching in the session plan and read the named passage aloud.
  4. Talk, 15 minutes. Two or three of the discussion questions. You don't need all of them.
  5. Write, 15 minutes. Quiet time on the worksheet. Men write. You write too.
  6. Commit and pray, 10 minutes. Each man reads his one commitment for the week aloud to his partner. Partners pray for each other. Close.

Pair every man before he leaves night one

The accountability is what makes this different from a Bible study with homework. Pair men up on night one, two by two, with one group of three if your number is odd. Pair men who don't already talk every day; old friends go easy on each other.

Read this agreement aloud and have each pair say yes to it:

"Once a week, on the night we pick, I'll text you one word about my commitment: done, or missed. If I haven't texted by the next morning, you'll ask me. When I miss, you won't preach. You'll ask: what's the plan for next time?"

That's all. It takes thirty seconds a week, and it's the part men tell you about months later.

A worked example

Here is how a hypothetical first night goes for a leader with nine men in a fellowship hall on a weeknight at 6:30. He has the room set with one table, nine chairs and the Step 1 worksheets face down. He opens with prayer at 6:30 sharp even though two men are still parking. There's no report on night one, so he uses those 15 minutes to have each man answer one question: "What promise have you made more than twice?" He teaches from the session 1 plan, asks two questions, and gives 15 quiet minutes to the worksheet. At 7:35 he pairs the men, reads the partner agreement, and each man reads his sentence to his partner. They pray in pairs and he closes at 7:45. Two men stay to talk. He lets them, but the session itself ended on time.

Check yourself before night one

  • You have a fixed day, start time and end time for all seven weeks
  • At least four men have said yes to you personally
  • Worksheets for session 1 are printed, one per man
  • You've decided how you'll pair men if the number is odd
  • You've done the Step 1 worksheet yourself, so you know what you're asking

If all five are true, you're ready. Open the session 1 plan and run it as written the first time. You can adapt it once you've seen how your men respond.
