Leader guide

Session 4: give each goal a time and a rule

What every man leaves with: his most important goal rewritten as a day, a time, a place and an action, plus a written line saying what counts as a miss.

Hand out: the Step 4 worksheet, "The laws of a system that holds," and the first Weekly Scorecard.

Read aloud: Luke 14:28 to 30, and Proverbs 21:5.

This is the session that changes things for most groups. Give the writing time its full 15 minutes even if the discussion is going well.

Report (15 minutes)

Each man says whether he caught his moment this week, and when: "Wednesday, 7:15, walking in the door." If he didn't catch it, that's a fine answer too. Write the times on a sheet as men say them. You'll use them in the teaching.

The teaching: four laws

Jesus says nobody builds a tower without sitting down first and counting the cost. A system is that counting, written down. Tonight we use four of its laws.

The clock law. Nothing exists until it has a time. "Weekly" is not a time. Thursday at 7:40 is. Across many studies, people who wrote down when and where they would act were measurably more likely to follow through than people who only decided to.

The source law. No source, no spend. Before your time, your money or your temper moves, name where it's coming from. The standard is scripture, not mood.

The failure law. Every system has a written way to fail. Decide what a miss looks like before you start, so one bad day doesn't cost you a month.

The evidence law. Numbers as numbers, not stories. Four out of seven is four out of seven, not "going well."

Worked example to read to the room

A man's goal is "phone away during dinner and bedtime." Last week he caught his moment: he walks in at 7:15 and checks his phone in the driveway before he comes inside.

His system: "Monday to Thursday, 7:15 PM, in the driveway, phone goes in the glove box until the kids are in bed."

His failure line: "A miss is any night the phone comes inside before 8:30. When I miss, I tell my wife that night and do it the next evening."

Notice that the time sits exactly where his wall was. That's the whole trick.

Discussion questions

  • Which tower have you started and left half-built?
  • Why does a written miss make it easier to keep going?
  • Where is your time going right now that nobody decided?

Commit and pray

Each man reads his system and failure line to his partner. Then he writes it on the Weekly Scorecard. The commitment: do it at the time you wrote, and fill in the Scorecard that night, every night, before next session. Text your partner your number once a week: something out of something.

Leader check

  • Every system has a day and a clock time
  • Every failure line describes something you could see
  • Every man has a Scorecard with his system written on it
  • Your own system is on your own Scorecard

From session 5 onward, the report is a number from the Scorecard. That's what makes the rest of the seven weeks work.
